How to use this calculator

This tool works two ways, depending on which problem you're solving:

  1. Calculate fees on total — enter the amount a client is paying you, choose the platform and their country, and see the fee taken out and what actually lands in your account.
  2. Reverse calculate — enter the amount you need to keep after fees, and the tool works out the total you should put on the invoice so the fee doesn't cost you anything.
  3. Select your country to load that region's default rate for each platform, in the local currency. Choose "Other / International" for any country not listed — you can type your own currency symbol and the tool falls back to a generic global rate estimate (use "Custom rate" below instead if you know the exact figures).
  4. If the client's card or account is issued in a different country than your own, tick "cross-border / international payment" to add the +1.5% surcharge Stripe and PayPal typically apply (Square and Wise don't carry a separate surcharge in this tool).
  5. Choose "Custom rate" to enter a processor's exact percentage and fixed fee instead — useful if you already know your negotiated or local rate.
  6. Results update instantly as you type — no button to press.
  7. Use "Copy invoice note" to grab a plain-text summary you can paste straight into an invoice or client email.

Why should freelancers pass payment processing fees to clients?

A processing fee is a cost of doing business tied directly to how you get paid, not to the work itself. When you absorb it, you're effectively giving every client a small, invisible discount that scales with invoice size and that they never asked for. Passing the fee through, or building it into your rate, keeps your take-home pay predictable regardless of which payment method or country a client is in. Many independent contractors state this plainly in their payment terms so there's no ambiguity: card and PayPal payments carry a processing surcharge, wire or ACH transfers typically don't.

How do Stripe and PayPal calculate international processing fees?

Domestic card rates are usually a percentage of the charge plus a small fixed fee, but both platforms add extra charges for cross-border transactions. Stripe and PayPal typically layer on an additional international or cross-border fee — often in the 1–2% range — on top of the base rate when the client's card or account is issued in a different country, and currency conversion adds a further spread if the payment isn't already in your local currency. This is why an invoice to an overseas client can cost noticeably more in fees than the same amount from a domestic one, and it's worth checking the current international rate on the processor's pricing page before quoting a client.

Tips for setting fair invoice terms for remote clients

Decide upfront whether your quoted rate already includes processing fees or whether they'll be added separately, and put that in writing before the project starts. Offering a lower-fee payment method, like a bank transfer or Wise, alongside card payments gives cost-conscious clients an option without you absorbing the difference. For recurring clients, consider naming the exact fee percentage in your contract so there's no back-and-forth on each invoice. Finally, round invoice totals to sensible amounts where possible; a total ending in an odd number of cents can look like a mistake even when it's the correct fee-adjusted figure.
